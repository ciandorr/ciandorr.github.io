Textbook

Most of the readings for the course will be in Joel Feinberg and Russ Shafer-Landau, eds., Reason and Responsibility, 12th edition (Wadsworth, 2005). Additional reading will be available on the web.

Recitations

You must be enrolled in one of the recitation sections for the course, which will be led by Chris Campbell and Cynthia Swinehart. Attendance and participation in recitation is required: discussing these topics with others is vital for deepening your own understanding and clarifying your own views.

To increase your incentive to attend the recitations, there will be an extra penalty for absences from recitation. You get two free absences; after that, each week's recitation you miss without a satisfactory excuse ('I was very busy that week' doesn't count!) counts for a sixth off your final grade. So if you miss six recitations, your final grade will be diminished by 4/6 = 2/3: if you would otherwise have scored a B+ for the course, you will be awarded a B-.

Grades and Assignments

NB: The following applies only to students who are not enrolled in a writing-intensive recitation. If you are in a writing-intensive recitation, the information about the assignments you will be required to complete and their importance for your final grade can be found in Jukka Keranen's section of the website.

Assuming you're not in a writing-intensive recitation, you will be required to write two papers---one 3 pages, the other 6 pages---and to take two exams. The first, 3-page paper will be due on Tuesday, February 1st. The midterm will be held on Tuesday, March 1st. The second, 4-page paper will be due on Tuesday, April 5th. The final exam will be held on Wednesday, April 27, from noon until 1.50 pm, in 324 CL.

Your final grade will be determined as follows, before the penalty for missing recitations (see above) is taken into account:
First paper 15%
Midterm 20%
Second paper 25%
Final exam 25%
Participation in recitation 15%

Late assignments

If you hand in an assignment late without a valid medical excuse or equivalent, your grade will be diminished by a third of a grade per day. So if you hand in a paper on Friday which was due on Tuesday, and your grade for that problem set would have been B+, it will instead be a C+.
