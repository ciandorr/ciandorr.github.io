Seminar on Time and Modality

Cian Dorr

Over the course of the semester I'll use this page to share course materials and readings, including excerpts from my book manuscript. Right now it's a bit bare-bones. Here's what I have:

For session 1:

  • The syllabus. This will stay here and perhaps be updated over the course of the semester.
  • A handout.

For session 2:

  • A handout.
  • Some excerpts from Counterparts, namely the first two subsections of chapter 1 as well as part of the last section, and part of one section from chapter 2 discussing a prominent psychological argument for propositional temporalism. Note: we didn't go as fast as I was intending, so some of the chapter 2 stuff will be discussed in session 3.
  • Chapter 2 of Ted Sider's book Four-Dimensionalism. Section 2.1 is especially relevant for this session.

For session 3:

  • Another batch of excerpts from Counterparts. This consists of most of section 2.3 and section 2.4.
  • Further readings: there's an epistemological argument against A-theories (or certain kinds of A-theories) that I'd like to talk about but that I haven't yet got around to writing about. A succinct presentation of the main thought is Braddon Mitchell's ‘How do we know that it is now now?’. There is useful criticism in Dan Deasy, ‘The Moving Spotlight Theory’. Also, Jeff Russell has an interesting new draft paper carefully discussing a version of the argument.
  • In the syllabus I said I'd single out some excerpts of Berit Brogaard's Transient Truths for this week's session, but didn't say which. Chapter 2 turns out to be the most relevant. You can find it on Oxford Scholarship.
